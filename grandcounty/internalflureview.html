Draft, not final Unincorporated county only Internal review

The Grand County Master Plan Update only addresses land in the unincorporated county. It does not have jurisdiction over, or address, land uses within town boundaries. The planning team is coordinating with each town to make sure the transition from town to county land use is considered appropriately. Everything you see here is a draft, and it will keep changing as review continues, so don't worry about getting it perfect.

Leaving a Future Land Use Suggestion

Four steps, start to finish. Nothing is saved until step 4, so feel free to look around first.

1

Turn on the Layers You Want to See

Open Layers and switch on Draft Future Land Use to see the current recommendation. Add Parcels or Public Lands if you want more context underneath it.

2

Find the Future Land Use Type in the Editor Widget

The Editor widget is already open on the left. Scroll through the list until you find the future land use type you'd recommend, then click it to activate point placement and the comment field.

3

Click the Map Where You'd Recommend a Change

Place your point on the parcel or general area you're referring to.

4

Add Your Comment and Save

Type a short comment explaining why you're recommending this change, then save. This is the part that helps the most, since it gives your suggestion context. Your point and comment are saved without a name or login attached.

Come Back Anytime

This is a draft in progress. Points and comments will help shape the next version of the map, so review as often as new areas come up for discussion.

Draft Future Land Use Categories

These are the categories you'll see in the Editor widget when you add a point. The color next to each one matches how it's drawn on the map.

Agriculture & Ranching Lands

Protect ranching and agriculture as both economy and community character; keep development very low density.

Open Space & Conservation

Public lands, conservation easements, wildlife corridors, and the open buffers that keep towns physically separate.

Large-Lot Residential

Large-lot, two-acre-minimum, single-family parcels that lack (and have no reasonable potential to gain) municipal water/sewer service, functioning as an “in-between” pattern that preserves distinct communities and rural character.

Small-Lot Residential

Existing platted subdivisions on individual wells and septic systems, generally under two acres, that predate current standards and lack realistic potential for municipal water and sewer service. Infill on existing lots is anticipated.

Neighborhood Residential

Town-adjacent land with existing or planned water and sewer service (or realistic potential for it), supporting moderate-density housing close to services and infrastructure.

Public, Civic, and Institutional

Identifies land in public, quasi-public, or institutional ownership that supports the delivery of government services, education, health care, emergency response, and community gathering.

Highway Commercial Corridor

Traveler and resident serving retail, dining, and services clustered at key nodes along US 40 and state highways. Designed to meet everyday commercial needs without becoming continuous strip development.

Resort & Accommodations

Land dedicated to visitor or tourism uses (hotels, resorts, or retreats) concentrated in appropriate locations to support tourism without spreading into residential neighborhoods.

Mixed Use Employment / Light Industrial

Areas set aside for job-generating uses beyond retail (light industrial, contractor yards, offices, mining), giving the local economy places to diversify.

Town

Not in County jurisdiction.

Draft categories and definitions — subject to change through the Master Plan Update review process.
